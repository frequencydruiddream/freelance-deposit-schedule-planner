# Freelance Payment Schedule Review Checklist

Use this checklist after you have scoped the work but before you send the proposal, statement of work, or contract. It helps you choose between a deposit, milestones, retainer, or invoice terms based on project risk, client approval process, and your own cash-flow needs.

## Confirm the project payment risk

- [ ] Identify whether the work is fixed-scope, ongoing, advisory, or open-ended.
- [ ] List the client-side approvals needed before work can begin.
- [ ] Check whether the client requires purchase orders, vendor setup, or specific invoice wording.
- [ ] Decide whether you need payment before reserving time, starting work, or handing over final files.
- [ ] Flag any work that would be hard to reuse, resell, or recover if the client delays payment.

## Choose the payment structure

- [ ] Choose an upfront deposit when you need commitment before blocking calendar time or starting production.
- [ ] Choose milestone payments when the project has clear phases, deliverables, or approval points.
- [ ] Choose a retainer when the client needs recurring access to your time or ongoing support.
- [ ] Choose invoice terms only when you are comfortable starting or delivering work before full payment.
- [ ] Avoid combining too many payment methods unless each one maps to a clear business reason.

## Define payment triggers and deliverables

- [ ] Name each payment event in plain language, such as deposit, design approval, draft delivery, launch, or monthly retainer.
- [ ] Write the deliverable or service period attached to each payment event.
- [ ] Specify whether payment is due before work starts, before the next phase begins, on delivery, or after invoice receipt.
- [ ] State what counts as client approval, including written approval, requested revisions, or no response by an agreed date if your contract allows it.
- [ ] Separate payment timing from vague project labels such as nearly done, final polish, or completion.

## Check invoice and contract clarity

- [ ] Match the payment schedule in the proposal, contract, and invoice instructions.
- [ ] Include the payment method, invoice recipient, purchase order requirement, and billing address if the client has provided them.
- [ ] State whether work pauses when an overdue payment blocks the next phase.
- [ ] Confirm who owns or receives final editable files, credentials, or production access before and after final payment.
- [ ] Remove language that promises unlimited revisions, undefined support, or delivery before required client inputs are received.

## Send and confirm

- [ ] Send the payment schedule with a short explanation of how it keeps the project moving.
- [ ] Ask the client to confirm any billing process that could delay the first payment.
- [ ] Wait for signed approval or written acceptance before beginning work that depends on the payment schedule.
- [ ] Create calendar reminders for invoice dates, milestone reviews, and follow-up points.
- [ ] Save the accepted payment schedule in the same place as the contract and project brief.
